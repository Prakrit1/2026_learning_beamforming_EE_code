\section{Purpose of this note}

This note separates three things that are easy to blur together when
presenting the idea: (i) what is taken directly from the reference paper
\cite{Schroder2025}, (ii) what we changed, and (iii) why the change is a
genuine departure and not a relabeling of the same optimum. The system and
channel model in Section~\ref{sec:system} is unchanged from the paper and is
restated only to fix notation. Section~\ref{sec:related} positions this
extension against the closest related work found outside the reference
paper's own line of work.

\section{System model (adopted, unchanged)}
\label{sec:system}

A set $\mathcal{M}=\{1,\dots,M\}$ of LEO satellites, each equipped with a
uniform linear array of $N$ antennas, serves a set $\mathcal{K}=\{1,\dots,K\}$
single-antenna users in the downlink. The channel between satellite $m$ and
user $k$ is $\mathbf{h}_{k,m}\in\mathbb{C}^{1\times N}$, stacked as
$\mathbf{h}_k = [\mathbf{h}_{k,1},\dots,\mathbf{h}_{k,M}] \in
\mathbb{C}^{1\times MN}$. Data symbols are precoded with
$\mathbf{w}_k \in \mathbb{C}^{MN\times 1}$ and superimposed,
$\mathbf{x} = \sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}} \mathbf{w}_k u_k$. The full precoding
matrix is $\mathbf{W} = [\mathbf{w}_1,\dots,\mathbf{w}_K] \in
\mathbb{C}^{MN\times K}$. The instantaneous achievable sum rate is

\begin{equation}
  R(\mathbf{W}) \;=\; \sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
  \log_2\!\left(1 + \frac{|\mathbf{h}_k \mathbf{w}_k|^2}
  {\sigma_{\text{noise}}^2 + \sum_{k'\neq k}|\mathbf{h}_k \mathbf{w}_{k'}|^2}\right).
  \label{eq:sumrate}
\end{equation}

CSIT is corrupted by two error sources acting on the true channel
$\mathbf{h}_{k,m}$: an angle-of-departure (steering) error
$\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{aod},k,m}\sim\mathcal{U}(-\Delta\epsilon_{\text{aod}},
\Delta\epsilon_{\text{aod}})$ and a synchronization phase error
$\epsilon_{\text{ph},k,m}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\text{ph}}^2)$, giving an
estimate $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{k,m}$ used by every precoder in place of the
true $\mathbf{h}_{k,m}$.

\section{The paper's optimization problem (reproduced)}
\label{sec:paper-problem}

The reference paper poses a single objective: maximize expected sum rate
under a per-satellite radiated-power budget,

\begin{equation}
  \max_{\mathbf{W}} \;\; \bar{R} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{H}}[R(\mathbf{W})]
  \qquad \text{s.t.} \quad \|\mathbf{W}_m\|_F^2 \le P/M \quad \forall m\in\mathcal{M}.
  \label{eq:paper-problem}
\end{equation}

Every precoder evaluated in the paper --- MMSE, robust SLNR, and the
learned SAC policy --- enforces this constraint by \emph{unconditional
rescaling} of the raw precoder to the boundary of the feasible ball:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{W}_{\text{paper}} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{P/M}{\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}}\|_F^2}}\;\mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}},
  \label{eq:paper-normalization}
\end{equation}

independent of $\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}}\|_F^2$. This is Eq.~(26)/(30) of the
paper for MMSE, and the SAC output transform is normalized identically. The
reward signal driving SAC's actor is the sum rate $R(\mathbf{W}_{\text{paper}})$
alone; power never appears as a cost, only as this hard, always-active
rescaling.

\begin{lemma}[Full-power optimality under rate-only objectives]
\label{lem:saturate}
Fix a precoding \emph{direction} $\hat{\mathbf{W}}$ with
$\|\hat{\mathbf{W}}\|_F^2=1$ and consider the scaled family
$\mathbf{W}(\alpha) = \alpha\hat{\mathbf{W}}$, $\alpha\in(0,\sqrt{P/M}]$. Then
$R(\mathbf{W}(\alpha))$ is non-decreasing in $\alpha$, so the maximizer of
\eqref{eq:paper-problem} over this family is $\alpha^\star = \sqrt{P/M}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Scaling $\mathbf{W}$ by $\alpha$ scales every signal term
$|\mathbf{h}_k\mathbf{w}_k|^2$ and every interference term
$|\mathbf{h}_k\mathbf{w}_{k'}|^2$ by $\alpha^2$, while
$\sigma_{\text{noise}}^2$ is fixed. Hence each user's SINR,
$\alpha^2|\mathbf{h}_k\hat{\mathbf{w}}_k|^2 /
(\sigma_{\text{noise}}^2 + \alpha^2\sum_{k'\neq k}|\mathbf{h}_k\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{k'}|^2)$,
is non-decreasing in $\alpha$ (strictly increasing while noise-limited,
saturating to an interference-limited ceiling as $\alpha\to\infty$, never
decreasing). $\log_2(1+\cdot)$ is monotone, so $R$ inherits the same
monotonicity. $\blacksquare$
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:saturate} is not a limitation of the paper's method --- it is
the \emph{correct} optimal behavior for problem~\eqref{eq:paper-problem}.
Rescaling every raw output to the boundary (Eq.~\ref{eq:paper-normalization})
is therefore not a modeling simplification; it exactly implements the
optimal solution structure of a rate-only objective. Any precoder solving
\eqref{eq:paper-problem} \emph{should} saturate the power budget.

\section{Our extension: making power a cost, not just a cap}
\label{sec:our-extension}

\subsection{Power consumption model (new)}

The paper's constraint counts only radiated power, $\|\mathbf{W}\|_F^2$. We
add a satellite HPA electrical power model of the same form used in recent
energy-efficient satellite precoding work \cite{HaEtAl2024}, splitting DC
power draw into amplifier draw and static circuit dissipation:

\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{total}}(\mathbf{W}) \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{tr}\{\mathbf{W}^H\mathbf{W}\}}{\eta_{\text{PA}}}}_{\text{amplifier draw}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{MN\,P_c}_{\text{circuit draw}},
  \label{eq:ptotal}
\end{equation}

with power-amplifier efficiency $\eta_{\text{PA}}\in(0,1)$ and per-antenna
static circuit power $P_c$. We set $\eta_{\text{PA}}=0.60$, matching the
satellite antenna/HPA efficiency used in \cite{HaEtAl2024} and consistent
with reported saturated TWTA/SSPA efficiencies of 55--70\%
\cite{Lohmeyer2016}. An earlier version of this model used
$\eta_{\text{PA}}=0.35$ sourced from a \emph{terrestrial}
macro/micro/pico/femto base-station power study \cite{Auer2011} -- not
satellite hardware -- and has been corrected. Two structural properties of
\eqref{eq:ptotal} matter for what follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No such term exists in the reference paper.} Its optimization
    problem \eqref{eq:paper-problem} has no notion of amplifier efficiency or
    circuit power; $P_{\text{total}}$ is our addition in full.
  \item $P_{\text{total}}$ has a strictly positive floor $MN P_c/\eta_{\text{PA}}\cdot 0 + MNP_c = MNP_c$
    as $\mathrm{tr}\{\mathbf{W}^H\mathbf{W}\}\to 0$: the circuit term never
    vanishes. This bounds the maximum achievable percentage power savings
    and keeps any rate/power ratio well defined even at zero transmit power.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The energy-efficiency objective (new)}

We replace \eqref{eq:paper-problem} with a fractional (concave-over-affine)
objective,

\begin{equation}
  \max_{\mathbf{W}} \;\; \mathrm{EE}(\mathbf{W}) = \frac{R(\mathbf{W})}{P_{\text{total}}(\mathbf{W})}
  \qquad \text{s.t.} \quad \|\mathbf{W}_m\|_F^2 \le P/M \quad \forall m\in\mathcal{M}.
  \label{eq:ee-problem}
\end{equation}

This is a genuinely different problem from \eqref{eq:paper-problem}, not a
relaxation of it: power now appears in the objective, not only in the
constraint. Lemma~\ref{lem:saturate} no longer applies, because scaling
$\mathbf{W}(\alpha)=\alpha\hat{\mathbf{W}}$ now moves \emph{both} the
numerator and the denominator:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{EE}(\alpha) = \frac{R(\alpha\hat{\mathbf{W}})}
  {\alpha^2\|\hat{\mathbf{W}}\|_F^2/\eta_{\text{PA}} + MNP_c}.
  \label{eq:ee-alpha}
\end{equation}

The numerator saturates (Lemma~\ref{lem:saturate}, bounded above by the
interference-limited ceiling); the denominator grows without bound, linearly
in $\alpha^2$. Consequently $\mathrm{EE}(\alpha)$ is not monotone: it
generically increases while noise-limited, then \emph{decreases} once the
marginal rate gained per additional watt falls below the marginal cost of
that watt. The maximizer $\alpha^\star$ of \eqref{eq:ee-alpha} is generically
in the \emph{interior} of $(0,\sqrt{P/M}]$, not at the boundary. This is the
precise sense in which the idea is not a relabeling of the paper's problem:
we did not merely permit $\|\mathbf{W}\|_F^2 < P/M$ (that permission alone is
vacuous under \eqref{eq:paper-problem}, by Lemma~\ref{lem:saturate}); we
changed the objective so the boundary stops being automatically optimal.

\begin{remark}
This is precisely the classical energy-efficiency/spectral-efficiency
trade-off \cite{Chen2011}: under a rate-only objective, more power is always
weakly better and the power cap binds; under a rate-per-power objective, the
optimal power level is an interior stationary point of a concave-over-affine
ratio, characterized by Dinkelbach's transform (Section~\ref{sec:schemes}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Relaxing the always-saturate precoder (new)}

To let the learned policy actually reach the interior of the feasible ball,
we replace the unconditional rescaling \eqref{eq:paper-normalization} with a
\emph{clip-only} projection,

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{W}_{\text{ours}} =
  \begin{cases}
    \mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}}, & \|\mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}}\|_F^2 \le P/M, \\[4pt]
    \sqrt{P/M}/\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}}\|_F \cdot \mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}}, & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:clip-only}
\end{equation}

Constraint (15b) of the paper is enforced exactly as written (an inequality)
rather than as the de facto equality every paper precoder produces. This is
the actual, literal feasible set of \eqref{eq:ee-problem}; \eqref{eq:paper-normalization}
only ever explores its boundary.

\section{Three formulations implemented and compared}
\label{sec:schemes}

\paragraph{Scheme I --- reproduction of the paper's convention.} Precoder
\eqref{eq:paper-normalization}, reward $R(\mathbf{W})/P_{\text{total}}$. Since
$P_{\text{total}}$ is a constant here (power is always exactly $P/M$), this
reduces to maximizing $R(\mathbf{W})$ alone --- it is the paper's sum-rate
objective in all but name, retained purely as a same-conditions reference
point.

\paragraph{Scheme II --- direct-ratio reward, free power.} Precoder
\eqref{eq:clip-only}, reward $R(\mathbf{W})/P_{\text{total}}(\mathbf{W})$
evaluated directly as a ratio. Numerically fragile as
$P_{\text{total}}\to MNP_c$ from below is bounded (Section~\ref{sec:our-extension}),
but the ratio's gradient can still be poorly scaled for online SGD.

\paragraph{Scheme III --- adaptively-priced reward, free power.}
Precoder \eqref{eq:clip-only}; reward uses the algebraic form of the
Dinkelbach transform \cite{Dinkelbach1967} --- $R(\mathbf{W})-\lambda
P_{\text{total}}(\mathbf{W})$ turns a ratio objective into a subtractive one,
which is what makes it usable as an RL reward at all --- but not the
Dinkelbach \emph{algorithm}:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{reward}_t = R(\mathbf{W}_t) - \lambda_t\, P_{\text{total}}(\mathbf{W}_t),
  \qquad
  \lambda_{t+1} = \frac{\overline{R}_{\text{window}}}{\overline{P}_{\text{total},\text{window}}},
  \label{eq:dinkelbach}
\end{equation}

where $\lambda_t$ is a running EMA of realized rate/power, updated every
transition, concurrently with a policy that is never frozen for any inner
solve. The classical scheme alternates: solve
$F(\lambda)=\max_{\mathbf{W}}[R(\mathbf{W})-\lambda P_{\text{total}}(\mathbf{W})]$
to exact global optimality for a \emph{fixed} $\lambda$, \emph{then} update
$\lambda$ from $F(\lambda^\star)=0 \iff \lambda^\star =
\mathrm{EE}(\mathbf{W}^\star)$. Neither half of that is what
\eqref{eq:dinkelbach} does. This is not a relaxation of Dinkelbach's method
in the sense of an inexact version of the same algorithm --- it is a
structurally different object (a continuously co-adapting price and policy)
that borrows the transform's algebra without either the exact inner solve or
the alternation. Section~\ref{sec:dinkelbach-rigor} treats it on those terms.

\section{What can and cannot currently be said about the online update}
\label{sec:dinkelbach-rigor}

An earlier version of this section tried to show that
\eqref{eq:dinkelbach} is a rigorously-justified \emph{inexact instance} of
Dinkelbach's method, via a three-condition inexact-fractional-programming
theorem from Jin et al.\ \cite{JinTangZhangWang2023}. Re-reading that paper
in full, and re-checking the codebase and the other cited results directly
rather than relying on how they are usually summarized, that argument does
not hold up. This section replaces it with what is actually true: two
structural facts that hold regardless of what the mechanism is called
(Sections~\ref{sec:bandit-fact}--\ref{sec:smooth-fact}), one honest but
weaker positive result (Section~\ref{sec:fixedpoint}), empirical evidence
bearing on --- but not proving --- whether training settles at all
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical-evidence}), and a direct statement of what
remains open (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

\subsection{Why \eqref{eq:dinkelbach} is not Dinkelbach's method, even inexactly}

Dinkelbach's method alternates: solve
$F(\lambda)=\max_{\mathbf{W}}[R(\mathbf{W})-\lambda P_{\text{total}}(\mathbf{W})]$
to exact global optimality for a \emph{fixed} $\lambda$, then update
$\lambda$ from that optimum. \eqref{eq:dinkelbach}'s active update path does
neither half: $\lambda$ updates every transition, unconditionally, via an
EMA with fixed window (constant blend factor
$2/(5000+1)\approx 4\times 10^{-4}$, never decayed), and the policy is never
frozen for any inner solve at any $\lambda$. This is a continuously
co-adapting price-and-policy system that borrows the transform's algebra,
not the algorithm.

Checking whether Jin et al.\ \cite{JinTangZhangWang2023} nonetheless covers
this: it does not. Their linear-convergence theorem (their Theorem 1) is
proven exclusively for a \emph{tabular} Fractional Q-Learning algorithm over
a finite state-action space, whose shrinking inner-loop error bound is
enforced by a tabular sub-routine (Speedy Q-Learning, a known-convergent
tabular method) with its own established convergence rate. The paper
separately proposes a deep-RL extension (D3QN + DDPG for their hybrid
discrete/continuous action space) for its experiments --- structurally the
closest thing to SAC anywhere in the paper --- but states no convergence
theorem for it; that extension is validated only empirically, via a results
figure, and the paper itself notes the resulting convergence curve "may
sometimes change non-monotonically." Mapping SAC onto the paper's three
conditions targets a theorem that was never proven for a continuous-action,
neural-function-approximated policy --- the paper leaves that case exactly
as open as this document does.

Two further citations require correction. This document previously
justified skipping the concurrent primal-dual RL literature
\cite{Paternain2019,DingZhangBasar2020} on the grounds that it "only"
applies to a softmax policy over a finite action space. Read directly,
neither paper says that. Paternain et al.\ \cite{Paternain2019} formulate
their constrained-RL setting over compact \emph{continuous} action spaces
from the outset, and separately prove their zero-duality-gap result extends
to neural-network policy parametrizations, with optimality loss "made
arbitrarily small" for near-universal approximators. The arXiv identifier
previously cited for Ding, Zhang, and Ba\c{s}ar (2020) in fact resolves to a
later, five-author journal extension \cite{DingZhangBasar2020} of that
conference paper; that extended paper's strongest, dimension-free rate is
for softmax specifically, but it separately establishes sublinear
convergence for "general smooth policy parametrizations" --- which would
include neural policies --- up to a function-approximation-error term.
Neither paper is a drop-in fit here (both are constrained RL, with a
Lagrange multiplier enforcing a hard constraint, not a Dinkelbach-style
ratio objective), but the stated reason for setting them aside was
incorrect, and on reflection they look like a more promising home for a
future rigor pass than the tabular result this section leaned on instead.

Finally, the two citations offered as "an explicit, checkable route" to a
shrinking approximation-error bound, Ghadimi--Lan \cite{GhadimiLan2013} and
Ghadimi--Lan--Zhang \cite{GhadimiLanZhang2016}, prove convergence of
projected stochastic gradient methods to a \emph{stationary point} of a
\emph{fixed, time-invariant} objective. Neither condition transfers:
stationarity does not imply near-global-optimality for a nonconvex
objective, and $f_\lambda(\mathbf{W})=R(\mathbf{W})-\lambda
P_\text{total}(\mathbf{W})$ genuinely is nonconvex in $\mathbf{W}$ ---
\texttt{calc\_sum\_rate.py} computes exactly the standard interference-coupled
SINR sum, the textbook source of MU-MIMO sum-rate's non-concavity, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth} below proves only smoothness, never convexity, in
$\mathbf{W}$. Both theorems also require $\lambda$ held fixed while the
optimization runs, which the active EMA never does.

Ha et al.\ \cite{HaEtAl2024}, by contrast, is accurately characterized here:
its Algorithm 1 genuinely alternates --- solve the inner problem, update
$\eta$, repeat to convergence --- the textbook Dinkelbach structure, and a
useful contrast case for what \eqref{eq:dinkelbach} is not. Deng et al.\
\cite{DengEtAl2023} is a real, correctly-attributed paper; its precise
inner-solve mechanism was not independently re-verified for this revision
and should be treated as unconfirmed rather than as supporting evidence.

\subsection{What holds regardless of the mechanism's name}
\label{sec:bandit-fact}

\begin{lemma}[Action-independent transitions collapse temporal credit assignment]
\label{lem:bandit}
Let $s_t$ denote the SAC state at training step $t$ and $a_t$ the
(pre-projection) action. If the state-transition kernel satisfies
$s_{t+1}\sim p(\cdot\mid s_t)$, independent of $a_t$, then for every state $s$,
$$
Q^\star(s,a) \;=\; r(s,a) \;+\; \gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{s'\sim p(\cdot\mid s)}\big[V^\star(s')\big],
\qquad
\arg\max_a Q^\star(s,a) \;=\; \arg\max_a r(s,a).
$$
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the Bellman optimality decomposition,
$Q^\star(s,a)=\mathbb{E}[r_t+\gamma V^\star(s_{t+1})\mid s_t=s,a_t=a]$. Since
$s_{t+1}\sim p(\cdot\mid s_t)$ does not depend on $a_t$, the second term
equals $\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{s'\sim p(\cdot\mid s)}[V^\star(s')]$ for every
$a$ --- a quantity constant in $a$. Adding an $a$-independent term to $r(s,a)$
does not change its argmax. $\blacksquare$
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[This holds by construction in this codebase, not by assumption]
\texttt{update\_sim()}, the state-transition routine shared by every training
script in this repository, advances user/satellite positions, rolls CSIT
estimation errors, and recomputes channel state information without ever
receiving the chosen precoder as an argument. The hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bandit} is therefore exact, not approximate: whichever
$\mathbf{W}_t$ SAC outputs, the channel realization at step $t+1$ is drawn
from the same exogenous process regardless. There is no delayed consequence
of a precoding decision anywhere in this environment --- it is a per-sample
stochastic optimization problem wearing an RL interface, not a genuine
sequential MDP.

A second, independent fact makes this stronger than the environment's
structure alone requires: \texttt{config\_sac\_learner.py} sets
\texttt{future\_reward\_discount\_gamma}$\,=0$, and \texttt{train\_graph}
gates its entire next-state-bootstrapping branch behind $\gamma>0$. With
$\gamma=0$ that branch never executes --- the critic's regression target is
literally \texttt{target\_q = rewards}, not a bootstrapped return. The
critic is not learning a discounted value function that Lemma~\ref{lem:bandit}
happens to simplify; by this configuration choice alone, and regardless of
whether the environment is action-independent, it is directly regressing
immediate reward. Lemma~\ref{lem:bandit} explains why that choice costs
nothing relative to the general (bootstrapped) case; it is not needed to
establish what the trained critic's target actually is.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What this does and does not license]
Lemma~\ref{lem:bandit} removes temporal credit assignment as a concern. It
says nothing about whether a concurrently-updated $\lambda$ converges to
anything in particular, nor about whether SAC's inner optimization (for
whatever $\lambda$ it currently faces) reaches a good solution. Those
questions are addressed, and left partly open, in
Sections~\ref{sec:fixedpoint}--\ref{sec:open}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Smoothness of the fixed-$\lambda$ reward surface}
\label{sec:smooth-fact}

For a fixed $\lambda$, $F(\lambda) =
\max_{\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{W}}[R(\mathbf{W})-\lambda P_{\text{total}}(\mathbf{W})]$
--- the value of the \emph{exact}, globally-solved inner problem --- is
convex in $\lambda$ on $\mathbb{R}$ (a pointwise supremum of functions affine
in $\lambda$ is convex, regardless of the form of $R$ or $P_{\text{total}}$).
This is a real property of the idealized optimization landscape and is
worth recording, but it characterizes $F(\lambda)$ itself, not anything SAC
computes: SAC never solves the inner problem to global optimality, so this
convexity-in-$\lambda$ fact does not, by itself, say anything about the
policy the training procedure actually reaches. The property that does
bear on the trained policy is smoothness \emph{in $\mathbf{W}$}, for fixed
$\lambda$:

\begin{lemma}[Smoothness of the per-step Dinkelbach surface]
\label{lem:smooth}
Fix $\lambda\ge0$ and let $f_\lambda(\mathbf{W}) = R(\mathbf{W}) - \lambda
P_{\text{total}}(\mathbf{W})$. On $\mathcal{W}$, $f_\lambda$ is continuously
differentiable with Lipschitz-continuous gradient (finite $L_\lambda$-smooth).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
$\sigma_{\text{noise}}^2>0$ is a fixed positive constant, so every SINR
denominator in \eqref{eq:sumrate} is bounded away from zero uniformly on
$\mathcal{W}$; $R$ is a composition of rational functions (bounded
denominator) with $\log_2(1+\cdot)$, hence real-analytic on $\mathcal{W}$.
$P_{\text{total}}$ \eqref{eq:ptotal} is affine in the quadratic form
$\mathrm{tr}\{\mathbf{W}^H\mathbf{W}\}$, with constant Hessian
$(2/\eta_{\text{PA}})I$. A finite linear combination of functions with
continuous Hessian has bounded (hence Lipschitz) Hessian on the compact set
$\mathcal{W}$. $\blacksquare$
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The clip is a projection, not an objective-level discontinuity]
The precoder \eqref{eq:clip-only} is exactly Euclidean projection
$\Pi_{\mathcal{W}}$ onto a convex ball. The composite map
$\mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}}\mapsto f_\lambda(\Pi_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}}))$,
viewed as a function of the unconstrained actor output, has a discontinuous
Jacobian exactly on the sphere $\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{raw}}\|_F^2=P/M$ ---
$\Pi_{\mathcal{W}}$ itself is what is non-smooth there, not $f_\lambda$. This
is the setting nonconvex \emph{projected}-stochastic-gradient theory is
built for \emph{when $\lambda$ is fixed}: convergence there is stated via
the gradient mapping $G_\lambda(\mathbf{W}) =
\big(\mathbf{W}-\Pi_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbf{W}+\alpha\nabla
f_\lambda(\mathbf{W}))\big)/\alpha$ rather than the raw gradient
\cite{GhadimiLanZhang2016}, sidestepping the projection boundary's
non-smoothness instead of requiring it away. Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth} is the
hypothesis on $f_\lambda$ that framework needs --- but the framework itself,
per \cite{GhadimiLan2013,GhadimiLanZhang2016}, additionally requires
$\lambda$ to be held fixed while it runs, and the active per-step EMA in
\eqref{eq:dinkelbach} never freezes $\lambda$. Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth} is
therefore evidence that gradient-based training is locally well-behaved
\emph{at any instant}, not a license to invoke this convergence framework
as stated.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A weak, honest positive result: self-consistency, not optimality}
\label{sec:fixedpoint}

One argument can be made without borrowing convergence machinery that
does not apply. Suppose the joint process $(\lambda_t,\theta_t)$ ($\theta$
the policy parameters) reaches a stationary point: $\lambda$ stops moving,
the policy stops changing. Two things are then true \emph{by construction},
not by assumption: $\lambda$ must equal the realized $R(\theta)/P_\text{total}(\theta)$
exactly, since $\lambda$ is defined as an EMA tracking that ratio and an EMA
of a constant sequence converges to that constant; and the policy must be a
(soft) maximizer of $R-\lambda P_\text{total}$, since that is what SAC's
training objective pushes toward for a stationary reward. Together these are
exactly Dinkelbach's fixed-point condition, $\lambda^\star =
R(\mathbf{W}^\star)/P_\text{total}(\mathbf{W}^\star)$ at a maximizer of
$R-\lambda^\star P_\text{total}$ --- so \emph{if} the dynamics settle
anywhere, they settle somewhere self-consistent with the Dinkelbach
optimality condition.

\begin{remark}[What this does not show]
This is a characterization of self-consistency, not of optimality, and it
is important not to overstate it. Dinkelbach's classical equivalence between
$F(\lambda^\star)=0$ and global ratio-optimality relies on $\mathbf{W}^\star$
being the \emph{global} maximizer of $R-\lambda^\star P_\text{total}$; here
$\theta$ is at best a local stationary point of a nonconvex training
objective. A policy that transmits at the circuit-power floor with near-zero
rate is, in this weak sense, just as much a "fixed point" as a good one ---
$\lambda\to 0$ and $R-\lambda P_\text{total}\approx 0$ are self-consistent
too. This argument establishes that $\lambda$ correctly describes whatever
the policy ends up doing; it says nothing about why the policy ends up doing
something good, which is a separate, much harder claim this document does
not make. It is also unclear the premise is ever exactly satisfied: both
optimizer learning rates in \texttt{config\_sac\_learner.py} are constant
(no decay schedule is active), and constant-step-size stochastic
optimization generically settles into a noisy neighborhood rather than a
literal fixed point.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Empirical evidence, honestly bounded}
\label{sec:empirical-evidence}

Two checks against an active training run (job with the current, reverted
\texttt{EE\_sac.py}, plain \texttt{lwin5000} mode) bear on, without proving,
whether the dynamics in Section~\ref{sec:fixedpoint} settle in practice.

\emph{Timescale separation is real but not asymptotic.} Episode-to-episode,
$\lambda$'s mean relative change is consistently about $4\times$ smaller
than that of the realized $R/P_\text{total}$ ratio it tracks, stable across
three non-overlapping windows spanning roughly $4000$ episodes (ratios
$4.03$, $3.91$, $4.03$). This is genuine evidence of practical separation ---
not asserted from the EMA window size, measured from training logs --- but
it is a constant ratio, not one diverging toward the
$a(n)/b(n)\to 0$ condition classical two-timescale stochastic-approximation
theory needs. $\lambda$'s and the realized ratio's episode means also track
each other almost exactly throughout (e.g.\ $2.679$ vs.\ $2.682$ early,
$3.299$ vs.\ $3.300$ late), which is closer to $\lambda$ being a smoothed
shadow of one signal than to two variables settling into separately-decoupled
equilibria.

\emph{The entropy-driven bias described below is not shrinking, and by one
measure is still growing.} $\alpha_\text{SAC}$ (the auto-tuned entropy
temperature) rises monotonically across the full range checked so far
(episode-window means $0.32\to 7.77\to 8.85\to 9.20$ over roughly $5100$ of
$13{,}000$ episodes), with shrinking but nonzero variance --- not the plateau
a first glance at a handful of adjacent episodes suggests. This is
consistent with, and does not contradict, the entropy-bias diagnosis below;
if anything it shows the bias is still actively evolving well into training,
not settled at a constant floor.

\subsection{The entropy-related bias, and what remains open}
\label{sec:open}

SAC's maximum-entropy objective introduces a real bias independent of all of
the above: the auto-tuned temperature $\alpha_{\text{SAC}}$ is adjusted to
hold the policy's entropy at \texttt{target\_entropy}, fixed at $1.0$ for
the entire run in \texttt{config\_sac\_learner.py} and never annealed in the
code this document currently describes. The soft-optimal policy therefore
maximizes $\mathbb{E}[Q(s,a)]+\alpha_{\text{SAC}}\mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot\mid s))$,
not $\mathbb{E}[Q(s,a)]$ alone, and the gap from the true argmax is bounded
by a term of order $\alpha_{\text{SAC}}$ that Section~\ref{sec:empirical-evidence}
shows is not shrinking. Annealing \texttt{target\_entropy} (or decaying
$\alpha_\text{SAC}$) is a plausible, minimally-invasive fix --- a training
schedule change, not an architectural one --- but whether it closes this gap
in practice, and whether closing it changes the trained policy's realized
$\mathrm{EE}(\mathbf{W})$, are both untested: an earlier pilot run with
annealing enabled was launched but its outcome was never checked before the
code was reverted to the non-annealed version described here.

A related, purely empirical stabilization idea is worth noting separately:
the $\lambda$ update in \eqref{eq:dinkelbach} is, mathematically, a pure
integral controller on the rate/power gap, known from control theory to be
prone to oscillation and overshoot. PID-Lagrangian methods from safe RL
\cite{StookeAchiamAbbeel2020} add proportional and derivative terms to
exactly this kind of update to damp such oscillation. This is a cheap
engineering fix for training stability; it comes with no convergence proof
of its own.

What would be needed for a genuine rigor result, beyond what is established
here: either (i) a local, KKT-style optimality characterization at a joint
stationary point (weaker than global optimality but provable, and a more
promising target than what was attempted before), or (ii) an algorithmic
change --- replacing the fixed-window EMA with a diminishing-step-size
update for $\lambda$ --- that would make the asymptotic separation classical
two-timescale theory needs actually hold, at the cost of $\lambda$ becoming
less responsive late in training. The corrected reading of
\cite{Paternain2019,DingZhangBasar2020} in
Section~\ref{sec:dinkelbach-rigor} above is the more promising literature
home for either direction than the tabular result this section originally
relied on. Neither is carried out here; both are left as concrete,
well-specified future work rather than as claims this document makes.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The reference paper \cite{Schroder2025} itself continues an earlier
single-objective (sum-rate only) line of work by the same group, starting
with the first application of SAC to this exact robust-precoding problem
\cite{GraclaICASSP2023} and its extension to a flexible multi-scenario
framework \cite{Gracla2024}, which together introduced the SAC-based
precoding framework this note builds on. The same group has also directly
compared RSMA against SDMA and OMA precoding on this system under CSIT
error \cite{SchroderRSMA2023}, though without an energy-efficiency
objective --- relevant given the RSMA framing of the codebase this note
documents, but a separate multiple-access question from the EE one this
note addresses. Outside that lineage, three adjacent bodies of work are
relevant, and it is worth being precise about what each does and does not
share with the extension proposed here.

\textbf{Energy-efficient beamforming via deep RL.} DRL has been applied
directly to EE-objective beamforming design, e.g.\ uplink beamforming for
long-term EE maximization in cell-free networks via DDPG
\cite{LiNiTianHua2021}. This confirms ``RL for an EE objective'' is an
established combination in general, but not in a satellite downlink setting
and not paired with CSIT-error robustness.

\textbf{Energy-efficient learning for LEO satellites.} DRL has also been
used for EE optimization specifically in LEO satellite systems, e.g.\ joint
beamforming, UAV positioning, and rate-splitting for RSMA-based LEO links
assisted by an active reconfigurable intelligent surface
\cite{YeganehBehroozi2025}, and a risk-aware (CVaR-based) DRL framework for
robust hybrid beamforming in massive-MIMO LEO systems that also targets
energy efficiency \cite{RiskAwareLEO2024}. The latter extends an earlier,
non-learned CVaR-based robust beamforming formulation by an overlapping
author group \cite{AlsenwiCVaR2023}, which solves the same risk-aware
hybrid-precoding problem via alternating optimization (no DRL) and targets
data-rate QoS under worst-case channel variation rather than an explicit
energy-efficiency objective. Together, these three combine EE, robustness,
and LEO satellites as we do, but in materially larger/different system
architectures (RIS- and UAV-assisted, or massive-MIMO hybrid precoding) and
via a different robustness mechanism (risk measures rather than training
under a CSIT error distribution).

\textbf{Fractional programming combined with DRL.} The Dinkelbach
transform's classical, non-learned use in beamforming is developed
comprehensively in \cite{ShenYu2018}. Closer to our combination,
\cite{JiaoFangDing2022} jointly uses fractional programming and DRL for
robust EE beamforming in a WPT-assisted D2D/MISO-NOMA setting; there,
fractional programming and DRL solve separate subproblems in an alternating
scheme, rather than the Dinkelbach multiplier being folded directly into a
single RL reward signal as in \eqref{eq:dinkelbach}.

\textbf{Closest classical (non-learned) analogue.} \cite{HaEtAl2024}
solves an EE precoding problem for a multi-gateway, bent-pipe SATCOM system
using Dinkelbach's method combined with an MMSE-based (WMMSE-style)
transformation and a compressed-sensing relaxation for sparse
feeder-link-beam matching -- the same general tool (Dinkelbach) applied to
a materially different, and more classically tractable, version of the
problem. Three differences separate it from this work: (i) it is a
model-based iterative convex optimization, solved assuming accurate channel
knowledge at every step, not a learned policy; (ii) it does not train
against or evaluate robustness to CSIT \emph{estimation error} -- channel
impairments (phase noise, fading) are simulated, but the algorithm optimizes
assuming it knows the channel it is given; and (iii) Dinkelbach's $\lambda$
is updated via the classical exact alternating scheme (solve the inner QCQP
to convergence, then update $\lambda$), not the online, continuously-updated
reward-embedded form of \eqref{eq:dinkelbach}. This sharpens the gap this
work fills: robustness to erroneous CSIT is difficult to fold into a
closed-form/convex-relaxation solver of this kind, which is precisely why
\cite{Schroder2025} argues for a learned policy in the first place -- an
argument this work extends from the sum-rate objective to the EE one.

None of these combine, on the exact system and CSIT-error-robustness setup
of \cite{Schroder2025}, (i) the electrical power model \eqref{eq:ptotal},
(ii) the resulting EE objective \eqref{eq:ee-problem}, and (iii) an
online, reward-embedded Dinkelbach update \eqref{eq:dinkelbach}. That
combination, on this system, is the specific gap this work fills.

The claim of novelty rests on Section~\ref{sec:our-extension}: introducing
$P_{\text{total}}$ into the objective is what breaks
Lemma~\ref{lem:saturate}'s always-saturate conclusion, and Section~\ref{sec:related}
situates that claim against the closest work found elsewhere in the
literature. Whether the trained policy \emph{empirically} finds and exploits
an interior optimum that beats Scheme~I on realized $\mathrm{EE}(\mathbf{W})$
is a separate, still-open empirical question, addressed by evaluation rather
than by the formulation itself. Section~\ref{sec:dinkelbach-rigor} addresses
a different open question --- not whether the trained policy performs well,
but whether the training procedure itself has a defensible theoretical basis
--- and, on correction, establishes less than an earlier draft claimed:
self-consistency of the $(\lambda,\theta)$ fixed point, not its optimality,
plus two concrete, not-yet-implemented directions (entropy-target annealing;
a diminishing-step-size $\lambda$ update) that remain untested rather than
a closed result.
