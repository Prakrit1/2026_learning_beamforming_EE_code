## TL;DR: the whole investigation, condensed

The investigation moved through several hypotheses (reward bugs, entropy
mechanism, network capacity) before landing on the one that actually
mattered: **this project used to train every model at
EE_TRAIN_ERROR_BOUND=0.5, an error range 10x larger than the reference
paper ever uses.** Retraining at realistic error levels (0.0, 0.025, 0.05,
matching the paper's own Fig. 3 training points) produced genuine power
savings (~56-63% of budget) and rate-vs-error curves with the paper's
expected shape (higher-error-trained models degrade more gracefully,
crossing over lower-error-trained models as error increases). Classical
baselines (MMSE/MRT/ZF), channel-correlation, and satellite-hardware
parameter cross-checks were all completed in the original repo (see that
repo's own now-superseded handoff, or `git log` there, for full derivations
-- summary tables below).

**Current "final" EE checkpoint set** (aod = training-time CSIT error
bound on additive_error_on_cosine_of_aod):
- aod=0.0: `EE_dinkelbach_adaptive_lwin5000_N16K3_eta0.6_rawpow` (default-LR)
- aod=0.025: `EE_dinkelbach_adaptive_aod0.025_lwin5000_N16K3_eta0.6_rawpow_lrc1.82e-06_lra1.17e-06` (tuned-LR, adopted after an Optuna LR search)
- aod=0.05: `EE_dinkelbach_adaptive_aod0.05_lwin5000_N16K3_eta0.6_rawpow` (default-LR)
- Reference/baseline models also used in comparisons: `full_EE_aod0.5_N16K3_eta0.6`
  ("regular", always-rescale-to-100W SAC, trained at the old error=0.5 regime --
  fine for spatial/beampattern comparisons, not for its own rate numbers) and
  classical MMSE/MRT/ZF (algebraic, no checkpoint).

**Beampattern robustness finding (previous session)**: EE checkpoints'
beams are narrower than the "regular" 100W checkpoint's, and that narrowness
directly costs coverage under CSIT error -- EE's beam only delivers ~52-57%
of its own peak gain at the true user angle on average (vs. ~72% for
"regular"), and that coverage ratio correlates strongly with achieved sum
rate (r~0.8-0.98 across every checkpoint tested). Root cause: CSIT error
here perturbs cos(AoD), not the angle itself -- error bound `b` maps to
roughly `b/sin(theta)` radians of real angular offset (error=0.05 -> ~2.95
deg), right in the same range as the EE beams' own -3dB beamwidth.

**Fairness reward term (this and previous session) -- the current active
thread.** Added a Jain's-fairness term to the aod=0.05 checkpoint's
Dinkelbach reward (`EE_FAIRNESS_WEIGHT` env var, reusing pre-existing
`calc_jain_fairness` from `src/data/calc_fairness.py`, previously wired
into several OLDER non-EE training scripts but never into EE_sac.py). Three
trial weights trained (0.5/1.5/3.0) and fully evaluated as of today -- see
"Fairness reward term: full results" below. **Key finding: the fairness
benefit does NOT keep growing with weight -- rate peaks at weight=1.5, not
3.0.** Weight=3.0 buys the highest fairness index and beam coverage but
starts trading away rate; weight=1.5 is the best rate/fairness tradeoff
found so far, not the highest weight tried.

## Fairness reward term: full results (all 4 aod=0.05 variants: baseline, fair0.5/1.5/3.0)

Mechanism (implemented in the original repo's EE_sac.py, carried over to
this repo's copy, unchanged): inside `energy_efficiency_dinkelbach_adaptive`'s
reward branch, `if 'fairness' in config.config_learner.reward: reward +=
weight * calc_jain_fairness(...)`, computed on the same physically-valid
clipped precoder/channel already used for the rate term. `EE_FAIRNESS_WEIGHT`
env var (default 0.0 = off, bit-identical to before) sets the weight and
appends a `_fair{weight}` training_name suffix.

**Rate/power/Jain's-index sweep, 11-point CSIT error 0.00-0.10** (script:
`fairness_evaluation.py` in the original repo, extended today to cover all
4 variants together in one run -- not yet copied to this repo, see
"Today's work" below):

| weight | rate @0.00 | rate @0.05 | rate @0.10 | power @0.00 | Jain's @0.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline (no fairness) | 1.95 | 1.79 | 1.26 | 56.3% | 0.725 |
| fairness=0.5 | 2.07 | 1.84 | 1.30 | 57.8% | 0.854 |
| fairness=1.5 | **2.10 (rate peak)** | **1.88 (rate peak)** | 1.35 | 61.4% | 0.923 |
| fairness=3.0 | 2.05 | 1.87 | **1.36 (rate peak)** | 65.5% | **0.954 (fairness peak)** |

Jain's index and power both climb monotonically with weight (expected).
Rate peaks at weight=1.5 across most of the error range; weight=3.0 trades
a bit of rate away for the highest fairness/power, except at the highest
error levels (>=0.09) where it edges back ahead. **So weight=1.5, not 3.0,
looks like the best overall rate/fairness tradeoff** -- this is the main
open decision (see "What to do next" below).

**Beampattern coverage-ratio quantification** (script:
`beampattern_robustness_analysis.py`'s `fairness_comparison` entry, all at
CSIT error=0.05, 30 realizations):

| model | mean beamwidth | mean coverage | median coverage | coverage<->rate corr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 2.71 deg | 0.564 | 0.646 | r=0.845 |
| fairness=0.5 | 2.89 deg | 0.598 | 0.649 | r=0.927 |
| fairness=1.5 | 3.24 deg | 0.656 | 0.690 | r=0.871 |
| fairness=3.0 | 3.64 deg | 0.697 | 0.771 | r=0.870 |

Beamwidth and coverage both increase monotonically with weight -- confirms
spatially that higher fairness weight makes the policy aim more forgivingly,
not just reallocate power numerically.

## 3GPP alignment decisions + pilot retrains launched (2026-07-27, same session as the 38.821 check above)

**Framing decision (user, explicit): "we are not comparing with Schroder's
paper, we are doing our own thing but the system scenario is based on that
-- we are leaning as close to 3GPP as we can."** Relevant because the
paper's own Table 3 (p. 10592) turned out to be the SOURCE of the old
config values: G_Sat=20 dBi and P=100 W are Schroder's numbers, not
unsourced guesses (the config's old "TODO: Wert nochmal checken" comment
notwithstanding). So old checkpoints = paper-matching parameters, new
checkpoints = 3GPP-matching; the two are intentionally different systems.

**EIRP-density analysis that drove the decisions** (definition: EIRP
density [dBW/MHz] = P_tx[dBW] + G_tx[dBi] - 10log10(BW_MHz); this code
never forms EIRP explicitly -- P_tx is the precoder norm, antenna gain
enters the channel PER-ELEMENT (config sat_ant_gain_linear = total/N,
los_channel_model.py), and the up-to-12 dB array factor emerges from the
precoder, so max total gain toward a user = the full sat_gain_dBi):
- Old system max EIRP density: 20 dBW + 20 dBi - 14.77 = 25.2 dBW/MHz --
  ~3 dB under Set-2 (28), ~9 dB under Set-1 (34).
- Both 3GPP sets imply the SAME ~75 W per-beam radiated power (Set-1:
  34+14.77-30 dBi = 18.8 dBW; Set-2: 28+14.77-24 = 18.8 dBW).
- **Set-1 chosen over Set-2** (user decision after the tension was
  spotted: our beamwidth matches Set-1, Set-2-matched EIRP would be
  internally inconsistent). Key physics: beamwidth in this sim comes from
  ARRAY GEOMETRY (16 el x 3lambda/2 = 3.4 m aperture, diffraction floor
  ~2.1 deg; learned beams 2.7-3.6 deg), NOT from sat_gain_dBi -- so
  aligning gain to Set-2 (24 dBi) could never make beamwidth match Set-2's
  8.83 deg (that would need a ~1 m array). Set-1 (2 m aperture, 4.41 deg,
  30 dBi) is the consistent column: with 30 dBi + 75 W, max EIRP density
  = exactly Set-1's 34 dBW/MHz.

## Process notes (still relevant)

- **sbatch only, always, no exceptions** -- and always via a real `.slurm`
  file, never `sbatch --wrap` (bit today's session: a `--wrap` invocation
  sourcing conda failed on `/bin/sh` not supporting `source`/bash-specific
  syntax; every working script in this project uses a real `.slurm` file
  for exactly this reason). This interactive shell sits directly on a live
  shared compute node outside SLURM's accounting.
- matplotlib gotcha: PlotConfig() resets text.usetex to True on
  construction; always matplotlib.use('Agg') + cfg.show_plots=False,
  re-asserted after constructing Config()/PlotConfig().
- SLURM job logs land at the REPO ROOT, not next to the .slurm scripts --
  in this repo, that's /home/parajuli/repos/2026_learning_beamforming_EE_code/*.out/.err.
  Original repo's own job logs stay in that repo's root, never here.
- CSIT error model perturbs `additive_error_on_cosine_of_aod`, NOT the AoD
  angle itself -- error bound `b` maps to roughly `b/sin(theta)` radians of
  real angular offset at our operating angles (~1.33-1.81 rad).
- Realization indices are only comparable WITHIN one gzip/job -- different
  beampattern_*.py scripts (or reruns of the same one) draw independent
  random realizations, so "realization 11" means different physical
  channels in different gzips.
- The reference paper PDF is at
  src/energy_efficiency/Model-Free_Robust_Beamforming_in_Satellite_Downlink_Using_Reinforcement_Learning.pdf
  (Schroder et al., IEEE Open J. Comm. Soc. 2025). Fig. 3 (N=16/K=3,
  d_K=100km) is the scenario this whole investigation targets.
