## TL;DR: the whole investigation, condensed

The investigation moved through several hypotheses (reward bugs, entropy
mechanism, network capacity) before landing on the one that actually
mattered: **this project used to train every model at
EE_TRAIN_ERROR_BOUND=0.5, an error range 10x larger than the reference
paper ever uses.** Retraining at realistic error levels (0.0, 0.025, 0.05,
matching the paper's own Fig. 3 training points) produced genuine power
savings (~56-63% of budget) and rate-vs-error curves with the paper's
expected shape (higher-error-trained models degrade more gracefully,
crossing over lower-error-trained models as error increases). Classical
baselines (MMSE/MRT/ZF), channel-correlation, and satellite-hardware
parameter cross-checks were all completed in the original repo (see that
repo's own now-superseded handoff, or `git log` there, for full derivations
-- summary tables below).

**Current "final" EE checkpoint set** (aod = training-time CSIT error
bound on additive_error_on_cosine_of_aod):
- aod=0.0: `EE_dinkelbach_adaptive_lwin5000_N16K3_eta0.6_rawpow` (default-LR)
- aod=0.025: `EE_dinkelbach_adaptive_aod0.025_lwin5000_N16K3_eta0.6_rawpow_lrc1.82e-06_lra1.17e-06` (tuned-LR, adopted after an Optuna LR search)
- aod=0.05: `EE_dinkelbach_adaptive_aod0.05_lwin5000_N16K3_eta0.6_rawpow` (default-LR)
- Reference/baseline models also used in comparisons: `full_EE_aod0.5_N16K3_eta0.6`
  ("regular", always-rescale-to-100W SAC, trained at the old error=0.5 regime --
  fine for spatial/beampattern comparisons, not for its own rate numbers) and
  classical MMSE/MRT/ZF (algebraic, no checkpoint).

**Beampattern robustness finding (previous session)**: EE checkpoints'
beams are narrower than the "regular" 100W checkpoint's, and that narrowness
directly costs coverage under CSIT error -- EE's beam only delivers ~52-57%
of its own peak gain at the true user angle on average (vs. ~72% for
"regular"), and that coverage ratio correlates strongly with achieved sum
rate (r~0.8-0.98 across every checkpoint tested). Root cause: CSIT error
here perturbs cos(AoD), not the angle itself -- error bound `b` maps to
roughly `b/sin(theta)` radians of real angular offset (error=0.05 -> ~2.95
deg), right in the same range as the EE beams' own -3dB beamwidth.

**Fairness reward term (this and previous session) -- the current active
thread.** Added a Jain's-fairness term to the aod=0.05 checkpoint's
Dinkelbach reward (`EE_FAIRNESS_WEIGHT` env var, reusing pre-existing
`calc_jain_fairness` from `src/data/calc_fairness.py`, previously wired
into several OLDER non-EE training scripts but never into EE_sac.py). Three
trial weights trained (0.5/1.5/3.0) and fully evaluated as of today -- see
"Fairness reward term: full results" below. **Key finding: the fairness
benefit does NOT keep growing with weight -- rate peaks at weight=1.5, not
3.0.** Weight=3.0 buys the highest fairness index and beam coverage but
starts trading away rate; weight=1.5 is the best rate/fairness tradeoff
found so far, not the highest weight tried.

## Fairness reward term: full results (all 4 aod=0.05 variants: baseline, fair0.5/1.5/3.0)

Mechanism (implemented in the original repo's EE_sac.py, carried over to
this repo's copy, unchanged): inside `energy_efficiency_dinkelbach_adaptive`'s
reward branch, `if 'fairness' in config.config_learner.reward: reward +=
weight * calc_jain_fairness(...)`, computed on the same physically-valid
clipped precoder/channel already used for the rate term. `EE_FAIRNESS_WEIGHT`
env var (default 0.0 = off, bit-identical to before) sets the weight and
appends a `_fair{weight}` training_name suffix.

**Rate/power/Jain's-index sweep, 11-point CSIT error 0.00-0.10** (script:
`fairness_evaluation.py` in the original repo, extended today to cover all
4 variants together in one run -- not yet copied to this repo, see
"Today's work" below):

| weight | rate @0.00 | rate @0.05 | rate @0.10 | power @0.00 | Jain's @0.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline (no fairness) | 1.95 | 1.79 | 1.26 | 56.3% | 0.725 |
| fairness=0.5 | 2.07 | 1.84 | 1.30 | 57.8% | 0.854 |
| fairness=1.5 | **2.10 (rate peak)** | **1.88 (rate peak)** | 1.35 | 61.4% | 0.923 |
| fairness=3.0 | 2.05 | 1.87 | **1.36 (rate peak)** | 65.5% | **0.954 (fairness peak)** |

Jain's index and power both climb monotonically with weight (expected).
Rate peaks at weight=1.5 across most of the error range; weight=3.0 trades
a bit of rate away for the highest fairness/power, except at the highest
error levels (>=0.09) where it edges back ahead. **So weight=1.5, not 3.0,
looks like the best overall rate/fairness tradeoff** -- this is the main
open decision (see "What to do next" below).

**Beampattern coverage-ratio quantification** (script:
`beampattern_robustness_analysis.py`'s `fairness_comparison` entry, all at
CSIT error=0.05, 30 realizations):

| model | mean beamwidth | mean coverage | median coverage | coverage<->rate corr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 2.71 deg | 0.564 | 0.646 | r=0.845 |
| fairness=0.5 | 2.89 deg | 0.598 | 0.649 | r=0.927 |
| fairness=1.5 | 3.24 deg | 0.656 | 0.690 | r=0.871 |
| fairness=3.0 | 3.64 deg | 0.697 | 0.771 | r=0.870 |

Beamwidth and coverage both increase monotonically with weight -- confirms
spatially that higher fairness weight makes the policy aim more forgivingly,
not just reallocate power numerically.

## 3GPP alignment decisions + pilot retrains launched (2026-07-27, same session as the 38.821 check above)

**Framing decision (user, explicit): "we are not comparing with Schroder's
paper, we are doing our own thing but the system scenario is based on that
-- we are leaning as close to 3GPP as we can."** Relevant because the
paper's own Table 3 (p. 10592) turned out to be the SOURCE of the old
config values: G_Sat=20 dBi and P=100 W are Schroder's numbers, not
unsourced guesses (the config's old "TODO: Wert nochmal checken" comment
notwithstanding). So old checkpoints = paper-matching parameters, new
checkpoints = 3GPP-matching; the two are intentionally different systems.

**EIRP-density analysis that drove the decisions** (definition: EIRP
density [dBW/MHz] = P_tx[dBW] + G_tx[dBi] - 10log10(BW_MHz); this code
never forms EIRP explicitly -- P_tx is the precoder norm, antenna gain
enters the channel PER-ELEMENT (config sat_ant_gain_linear = total/N,
los_channel_model.py), and the up-to-12 dB array factor emerges from the
precoder, so max total gain toward a user = the full sat_gain_dBi):
- Old system max EIRP density: 20 dBW + 20 dBi - 14.77 = 25.2 dBW/MHz --
  ~3 dB under Set-2 (28), ~9 dB under Set-1 (34).
- Both 3GPP sets imply the SAME ~75 W per-beam radiated power (Set-1:
  34+14.77-30 dBi = 18.8 dBW; Set-2: 28+14.77-24 = 18.8 dBW).
- **Set-1 chosen over Set-2** (user decision after the tension was
  spotted: our beamwidth matches Set-1, Set-2-matched EIRP would be
  internally inconsistent). Key physics: beamwidth in this sim comes from
  ARRAY GEOMETRY (16 el x 3lambda/2 = 3.4 m aperture, diffraction floor
  ~2.1 deg; learned beams 2.7-3.6 deg), NOT from sat_gain_dBi -- so
  aligning gain to Set-2 (24 dBi) could never make beamwidth match Set-2's
  8.83 deg (that would need a ~1 m array). Set-1 (2 m aperture, 4.41 deg,
  30 dBi) is the consistent column: with 30 dBi + 75 W, max EIRP density
  = exactly Set-1's 34 dBW/MHz.

### Reconsidering the deterministic-lambda mechanism (user decision, 2026-07-30)

Compared the fix's own hard-replace-every-episode update (`lambda_ee =
det_rate_sum / det_power_sum`, no memory of the previous value) against
the OLD mechanism's continuous per-step EMA (`lwin5000`, a 5000-*step*
smoothing window) -- structurally different, not just "less noisy."
User chose: **keep evaluating every episode (still noise-free, 512
samples) but EMA-smooth the update instead of hard-replacing it**, over
alternatives of (a) evaluating every N episodes (saves compute, doesn't
fix jitter, adds staleness) or (b) both.

**Implementation, all in `src/models/EE_sac.py` (matches the file that
already owns the reward/lambda logic per the 2026-07-29 session's
instruction):**
- New optional env var `EE_LAMBDA_DET_EMA_ALPHA` (float; unset = disabled
  = original hard-replace behavior, fully backward compatible with the
  four completed detlam checkpoints). When set, each per-episode
  deterministic-eval update blends: `lambda_ee += alpha * (new_estimate -
  lambda_ee)` instead of a hard jump. The very first update still hard-
  sets (nothing to blend with lambda's 0.0 init).
- Checkpoint folder gets an `_ema{alpha}` suffix appended after
  `_detlam{steps}[e{every}]`, so smoothed and unsmoothed runs never
  collide.
- Log line now prints the alpha in use.
- **Also added, same session: warm-start.** New optional env var
  `EE_WARM_START_MODEL_PATH` -- if set, loads the SAVED checkpoint's
  policy weights into `sac.networks['policy'][0]['primary']` right after
  `SoftActorCritic()` construction (network is already built/compiled by
  then), then calls `sac.update_target_networks(tau_target_update_
  momentum=1.0)` to hard-copy into the policy target too (matching
  `__init__`'s own initial hard copy). **Only the actor is seeded --
  `save_model_checkpoint()` only ever saved the policy 'primary' network's
  weights (see its own comment), so critics/target-value-networks/
  optimizer state have nothing saved to load and start fresh regardless.**
  Checkpoint folder gets a `_warmstart` suffix.
- Motivation for warm start: the aod=0.0 checkpoints showed clear evidence
  of undertraining (lambda/rate still rising) -- continuing from the
  existing policy rather than restarting from scratch should reach the
  new (much higher) lambda regime faster within the same 13,000-episode
  budget.

### Formulation-doc re-read: the "bug" may be a documented design choice

User asked to re-read `docs/EE_formulation.tex`'s RL/Dinkelbach section
closely and compare against what EE_sac.py actually implements. Key finding:

**The doc's own Dinkelbach update** (Eq. labeled `dinkelbach`, Sec 5):
lambda_{t+1} = R-bar_window / P-bar_total,window, computed from a RUNNING
WINDOWED AVERAGE OF THE ACTUAL ROLLOUT stream -- i.e., R(W_t) and
P_total(W_t) from whatever precoder SAC actually output at training step t,
under exploration noise. The doc explicitly frames this as "a deliberate
relaxation for the online RL setting" of textbook Dinkelbach. **This is
almost exactly the ORIGINAL `lwin5000` mechanism** (5000-step EMA over
rollout samples) -- the one the 2026-07-29 session decided was buggy and
replaced with `detlam` (a structurally different estimator: periodic
noise-free deterministic-policy evaluation, decoupled from the training
rollout entirely).

**The doc's own diagnosis of why the rollout-based lambda looks biased**
(Sec 6.3, "The one remaining gap: an unshrinking entropy bias"): SAC's
`target_entropy` is fixed at 1.0 for the ENTIRE 13,000-episode run (never
annealed) -- `config_sac_learner.py:59`, confirmed still true, no annealing
schedule existed anywhere in the codebase before this session. Non-annealed
exploration is exactly what inflates the rollout-measured power used in
lambda. But the doc's prescribed fix is: *"Annealing target_entropy (or
decaying alpha_SAC) over training turns this floor into a term that
vanishes as training proceeds... a schedule change to the existing training
loop, not an architectural one."* **None of the sessions from 2026-07-27
through 2026-07-30 tried this** -- all the engineering effort went into
changing lambda's estimator (detlam, then EMA+warmstart on top of detlam)
instead. The doc's Sec 6 rigor argument (convexity/smoothness/bandit-
structure lemmas) is built specifically around the online rollout-window
form of the update; it doesn't cover the deterministic-eval variant, which
has no theoretical backing in this doc. **Reframing: the "over-transmission
bug" may not be a mechanism-level bug at all -- it may be the documented,
anticipated entropy-floor artifact, with an already-specified fix
(entropy annealing) that had never been tried.**

### target_entropy annealing implemented

**`src/models/algorithms/soft_actor_critic.py`**: `self.target_entropy` was
a plain Python float, read inside `train_graph()` (which is
`@tf.function`-decorated). This is a real footgun: a plain float gets baked
into the traced graph as a CONSTANT at first call -- mutating it later would
have silently had NO EFFECT on training, only on what gets logged. Changed
to a `tf.Variable` (mirroring how `log_entropy_scale_alpha` already had to
be one, for the same reason), plus a new `set_target_entropy(value)` setter
using `.assign()` (which DOES reach an already-traced graph, since Variables
are captured by reference).

**`src/models/EE_sac.py`**: new env-gated linear schedule, off by default
(bit-identical to before if unused):
- `EE_TARGET_ENTROPY_ANNEAL_END` -- presence enables it (gate, mirrors
  `EE_DINKELBACH_LAMBDA_FIXED`'s presence-as-sentinel pattern)
- `EE_TARGET_ENTROPY_ANNEAL_EPISODES` -- episodes to anneal over (default:
  full run)
- Start value = current `target_entropy` (config default 1.0, or
  `EE_TARGET_ENTROPY` if also set)
- Applied once per episode via `sac.set_target_entropy(...)`, held at the
  end value after annealing completes; checkpoint folder gets a
  `_tanneal{start}to{end}[over{episodes}]` suffix; per-episode log line now
  also prints `target_entropy` alongside `entropy_scale_alpha`.

**Smoke-tested (job 155670, `smoke_test_target_entropy_anneal.py`/.slurm)**:
deliberately extreme schedule (1.0 -> -50.0 over 2 episodes) to make the
effect unambiguous. Result was decisive: episode 0
`entropy_scale_alpha=0.9933` (target_entropy=1.0, unchanged), episode 1
`entropy_scale_alpha=0.0021` (target_entropy=-50.0, crashed immediately),
episodes 2-3 fully collapsed to 0.0000. Proves the tf.Variable fix actually
reaches the compiled graph, not just that training doesn't crash.

### Side experiment: power-conditioned reward mode (user-proposed, implemented + piloted)

User's idea: instead of pricing power inside the reward (Dinkelbach's
lambda), give the power BUDGET to the actor as an extra state input
(sampled fresh per transition), always rescale the precoder to EXACTLY that
budget (Scheme I's always-rescale logic, generalized from a hardcoded
constant to a variable input), reward = pure rate, no power term at all.
Rationale discussed at length: this doesn't eliminate the rate/power
tradeoff, it RELOCATES it -- the network learns the rate-maximizing frontier
r*(H,P) for every budget in one pass; finding the EE-optimal P becomes a
cheap OFFLINE grid search over a converged, non-training network afterward
(`EE(P) = E[r*(H,P)]/P_total(P)` on a grid), instead of a live concurrently-
trained multiplier -- sidesteps every lambda-instability issue this project
has hit (undertraining, rollout-noise-inflation, entropy bias, EMA/warm-
start tuning) by construction, since there's no in-loop lambda at all. Open
risks flagged: harder representation problem (network must generalize
across P, not just one fixed point), reward-scale dynamic range across P
needs care, P-sampling distribution matters (log-uniform chosen over
uniform since the EE-optimal region is likely well below the 75W budget,
per the power-scaling-diagnostic finding of ~15-20W optima). This is a SIDE
experiment run ALONGSIDE the Dinkelbach/entropy-anneal thread, not a
replacement for it.

**Implementation, `src/models/EE_sac.py`**: new reward mode
`EE_REWARD_MODE=energy_efficiency_power_conditioned`. Env vars
`EE_POWER_COND_MIN_WATT` (default 1.0), `EE_POWER_COND_MAX_WATT` (default =
`power_constraint_watt`), `EE_POWER_COND_SAMPLING` (`uniform` or
`log_uniform`, default `log_uniform`). Mechanism: `network_args['size_state']`
bumped +1 (power feature); a per-env `power_cond_watt_list` is threaded the
same way `state_next_list` is -- sampled and appended (normalized into
roughly [-1,1] analytically from the KNOWN sampling distribution, no
empirical norm-factor pass needed) whenever a new state is built, read back
(BEFORE being resampled for the next state) when computing that state's
reward, so the budget the actor saw when choosing an action is the same one
its reward gets evaluated against. Reward branch mirrors Scheme I's
always-rescale logic but rescales to the sampled budget instead of the
config constant, and is pure `calc_sum_rate` (no power term). New per-
episode diagnostic log line: "Power-conditioned: episode mean sampled
budget X W ..., mean achieved rate Y". Checkpoint folder suffix:
`_pcond{min}to{max}{sampling}`. Training_name:
`EE_power_conditioned{...}_pcond1to75log_uniform` pattern. All changes
gated behind the new reward mode; every other mode is bit-identical.
